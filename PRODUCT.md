# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences share one page: (1) engineering peers, hiring managers, and potential collaborators evaluating technical/creative-dev craft, and (2) people encountering the fine-art side (oil painting, mixed media) who may have no engineering context at all. Visitors arrive with no prior relationship to the person — the site is doing first-impression work, not workflow support. There's no direct ask (no "hire me" CTA driving the design); the goal is reputation and network, so the site should reward exploration and be memorable/shareable rather than optimize for conversion.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for a full-stack/product engineer who is also a practicing artist (oil painting, mixed media, creative technologist, fine arts). It exists to hold both identities on one site without either one flattening the other: the engineering work should read as technically serious, and the art should read as genuine studio practice, not a "fun side project" footnote. Success looks like a visitor coming away with a strong, specific impression of who this person is and wanting to follow their work, not a completed contact-form submission.

## Brand Personality

Bold & experimental. The site should feel like an artist's studio that happens to be built by an engineer who's very good at motion — confident, a little raw, unafraid of intensity (aggressive display type, saturated accent, dark base) rather than polished-safe. Voice is direct and understated in copy, loud in visual/motion craft. Personality should come through in how things move and how type is handled, not through cute microcopy.

## Anti-references

- Sterile corporate/agency sites — anything that reads as a brochure, overly buttoned-up, or safety-first. No stock "available for hire" badges, no generic trust-signal rows.
- The generic dev-portfolio template look: uniform card grids, gradient text, bento layouts, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows, numbered section markers used as decoration rather than real sequence.
- Anything that treats the fine-art work as a decorative aside (a small "hobbies" section) rather than a first-class body of work with its own visual weight.

## Design Principles

1. **Two crafts, one voice** — engineering rigor and painterly instinct should both be visible in the same interactions (e.g. motion that feels hand-timed, not template-timed).
2. **Show, don't badge** — credibility comes from the work and the craft of the site itself, not labels, logos-of-employers rows, or testimonial carousels.
3. **Intensity over safety** — when in doubt, push the bold/experimental direction further rather than sanding it down toward convention.
4. **Studio, not showroom** — layout and pacing should feel like walking through someone's actual practice (rougher edges, real sequence) rather than a polished sales deck.
5. **Motion as authorship** — animation (GSAP/Lenis-driven reveals, cursor, transitions) is a primary expressive tool, not garnish; it should feel intentional and specific to each section rather than one reflex applied everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard baseline: WCAG AA color contrast, full keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored throughout. Full motion is the default experience — reduced-motion fallbacks only reach visitors who opted in at the OS level, so the brand's motion identity is untouched for everyone else. No additional accommodations specified beyond this baseline.
